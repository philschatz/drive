# The Salt House — outline

A gothic mystery in a fog-bound harbour town. Working title, working everything.
Outline as it stands on {{today}}.

> She had been told the house was empty. The house had been told otherwise.

## Premise

A **records clerk** returns to the harbour town she left at nineteen to settle her aunt's
estate, and finds the tide-house at the end of the breakwater has been kept — *swept, aired,
the lamps trimmed* — by someone who is not supposed to exist.

The mystery is not who is living there. It is why every official record of the house stops
in the same year, and who went to the trouble.

---

## Act one — arrival

1. She arrives on the late ferry, in fog, and cannot find the solicitor's office
2. The aunt's cottage: forty years of hoarded paper, all of it *almost* useful
3. First walk out along the breakwater — the tide-house, lamps lit, no one answering
4. The harbourmaster is helpful about tides and evasive about everything else
5. She finds the aunt's ledger, and one page has been cut out with a blade

## Act two — the records

The middle is where this either works or collapses. The engine is her job: she is *good*
at records, so the town has to be bad at hiding things in them.

1. The parish register, and the year that appears twice
2. A neighbour who remembers the family and then, the next day, does not
3. Break-in at the cottage — nothing taken except paper
4. The lighthouse keeper's daughter, now eighty, who says the wrong name and corrects herself
5. She gets inside the tide-house. Someone has been sleeping there for years.
6. **Midpoint:** the cut-out ledger page turns up in the tide-house, in her aunt's hand

Second half of act two is the town closing ranks, and her realising the aunt was not
investigating this. The aunt was *maintaining* it.

---

## Act three — the tide

1. The storm, and the breakwater cut off
2. What is actually in the cellar below the waterline
3. The choice: publish the record, or keep it the way three generations kept it
4. She chooses, and the choice costs her the town

I do not know yet which way she chooses. That is fine for now — I want to write toward it
rather than decide it in an outline.

---

## People

- **Ines Varela** — the clerk. Careful, literal, funny in a dry way she does not know is funny.
  - Wants: the estate settled and a ferry ticket out
  - Needs: to stop treating other people as unreliable records
- **The aunt (Ilda)** — dead before page one, present on every page
  - Kept the house. Cut the page. Left the ledger where it would be found.
- **Rui, the harbourmaster** — decent, complicit, tired
  - Not a villain. Just someone who made a call in his twenties and never revisited it.
- **The keeper's daughter** — the only person who will say anything straight
- **Whoever is in the tide-house** — on the page as little as possible until act two

## Setting notes

The town is the antagonist, so it needs specifics, not atmosphere:

- Fog that comes in at a *predictable hour*, which the locals plan around and she does not
- The breakwater walk, cut off at high water — timing is a plot device, not scenery
- Harbour records kept in a damp room, half of them illegible, which is *convenient* for someone
- A working town: ice plant, chandlery, two churches, one of them shut

## Things to research

- How parish registers and harbour records actually worked, and who had access
- Tide tables — enough to make the cut-off timings honest
- What a tide-house is for, and whether the cellar idea survives contact with reality
- Probate in the period, because the whole plot hangs off an estate

## Rules I am giving myself

- No fog on the first page. Everyone does fog on the first page.
- The supernatural stays *arguable* — every uncanny thing has a mundane reading
- She solves it by reading, not by being chased
- If a scene is only atmosphere, cut it

> Write the harbour first. The ghost can wait.
